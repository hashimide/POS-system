What is a POS?

POS means Point of Sale: the software a business uses at the moment a customer pays. It is the till, plus the bookkeeping behind it.

The core flow

1. Pick itemsScan a barcode, search, or tap a product.
→
2. Build cartQuantities, weights, discounts, tax.
→
3. PayCash with change, or card, bank transfer, wallet.
→
4. Save saleOne safe transaction in the database.
→
5. Update stockItems sold are deducted.
→
6. ReceiptPrinted or shown on screen.

Everything else in a POS is built around these six steps.

The main parts

PartWhat it does
CatalogProducts, variants, categories, prices, barcodes, units (piece, kg, dozen).
Checkout screenThe cashier's main screen: cart, quantities, discounts, payment.
PaymentsCash with change, card, bank transfer, wallets, split payments, refunds.
InventoryTracks stock, deducts on sale, blocks sale at zero, warns when low.
ReceiptsItemised, with tax and a unique receipt number.
Users and rolesCashier, manager and admin with different permissions.
CustomersOptional: customer records, purchase history, credit (udhaar), loyalty.
Shifts and cash drawerOpening and closing cash counts so the till balances at day end.
ReportsDaily sales, best sellers, sales per cashier, low stock, profit.

What makes POS software tricky

Money must be exact

No floating-point numbers. Totals, tax and change must always add up to the paisa.

Never lose a sale

A sale is saved fully or not at all. Power cuts and double clicks must not corrupt data.

Speed

A queue of customers is waiting. Searching, scanning and paying must feel instant.

Audit trail

Sales are never silently edited. Mistakes are fixed by voids and refunds that record who and why.

Pakistan considerations

Please verify before relying on this section. This is general background, not legal or tax advice. Rules and rates change. Confirm current details with an accountant or the official authority (FBR for federal tax, the provincial revenue authorities for provincial tax) before you build anything that depends on them.

Currency and rounding

PKR, written “Rs”. One rupee is 100 paisa. We store money as an integer number of paisa. In everyday cash trade, paisa coins are not used, so shops usually round the final cash amount to the nearest rupee. The system should support a cash rounding rule that is recorded on the receipt as a separate line.

Sales tax

Goods generally attract federal sales tax (GST) at a standard rate (commonly 18%, but check the current rate), some items are exempt or taxed differently, and services are taxed by the provinces at their own rates. So the system needs several tax rates, assignable per product, with a shop-wide choice of tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive prices.

FBR POS integration

Certain categories of retailers are required to connect their POS to FBR so that each invoice gets an official invoice number (and a QR code on the receipt). Whether this applies depends on the kind and size of business. It is an important reason to choose the target business early, because an FBR integration is a separate piece of work and may involve registration steps. Check requirements and any costs before promising it.

Payment methods

Cash, debit/credit card, bank transfer (including Raast), and mobile wallets such as JazzCash and Easypaisa are common. With a zero budget we record these as payment methods with a reference number. We do not integrate with gateways or terminals (those cost money).

Language

Many shops think and talk in Urdu. Plan for Urdu product names (stored as normal UTF-8 text), and possibly bilingual receipts and right-to-left layout. Decide early if this is required, because it affects every screen.

Units sold loose

Kilograms, grams, litres, metres, dozens and packs are all common. This is why weighted/decimal quantities and a units table are in the design from the start.

Customer credit (udhaar)

Selling on credit and keeping a ledger per customer is common in many local shops. It is not in version 1, but the customer and payment tables are designed so a “credit” payment method and a customer balance can be added later without rewriting sales.

Power cuts

Load-shedding is a real risk. Every sale is saved in a single database transaction, so a power cut cannot leave a half-saved sale. Recommend a UPS for the shop PC, and automatic backups before any real use.

Architecture

One Laravel application serves both the cashier screen and the back-office. There is no separate API server at the start. If we later need a mobile app or an offline client, we add a JSON API on top of the same business logic.

Where the business logic lives

Controllers stay thin. Rules that matter (completing a sale, refunding, adjusting stock) live in Action classes, so they are reusable and easy to test without a browser.

Controllers

Receive the request, call an Action, return a page or a redirect. No calculations here.

Actions

One class per business operation. Each runs inside a database transaction and is covered by tests.

Models

Describe tables and relationships. Never trusted for totals: totals are always recomputed on the server.

Core rules (non-negotiable)

These are the rules that stop a POS from becoming untrustworthy. Anything that breaks one of them is a bug.

Money is an exact integer

Store amounts as whole paisa (price_cents in the draft schema, 1 rupee = 100). Never floats. Format for display only at the edge.

Quantities are exact decimals

Quantities use numeric(12,3) in PostgreSQL (for example 1.250 kg). In PHP use exact-decimal maths (BCMath or a decimal library), not floats.

Sales are never edited or deleted

A mistake is corrected by a void or a refund, each its own record with a user and a reason.

Snapshot prices on the sale

sale_items copies the product name, variant name, unit and unit price at the time of sale. Changing a price later never changes an old receipt.

Stock is a ledger

Every change goes through a stock_movements row (sale, refund, purchase, adjustment). The stock total on the variant is a cached sum, updated in the same transaction.

Selling is all-or-nothing

Sale, items, payments and stock movements are saved in one database transaction. If any step fails, nothing is saved.

Block the sale at zero stock

If requested quantity is more than available stock, the sale is refused. Product rows are locked while checking, so two cashiers cannot sell the last item together. (Items marked “do not track stock” are exempt, for example services.)

No double submissions

The checkout request carries a unique key. A double-click or a retry can never create two sales.

The server never trusts the browser's totals

The browser sends items and quantities. The server finds the prices and works out all totals itself.

Everything sensitive is attributable

Voids, refunds, price changes, stock adjustments and large discounts record who did it and when.

No secrets in Git

The repository is public. No .env, no keys, no real customer data, ever.

Rounding rule (proposed)

Round each line to the nearest paisa (half up) after multiplying price by quantity. Sum the lines. Then apply tax rules. Document the rule once, in one helper, and use it everywhere.

Why this matters

If the checkout screen, the receipt and the report each round a bit differently, the books never match. One Money helper and a test for every example above prevents that.

Open detail: whether to round per line or per invoice, and how cash rounding is shown on the receipt, depends on tax rules for the chosen business. Decide after the tax question is answered.

Variants and weighted items

Both are decided as needed, and both change the database design, so they are built in from the start rather than bolted on.

Weighted and loose items

  • Each product has a unit (pcs, kg, g, litre, dozen). The price is per that unit, for example Rs 480 per kg.
  • Units say whether decimals are allowed. Pieces: whole numbers only. Kilograms: three decimals (grams).
  • The cashier types or enters the weight. A connected scale can come later.
  • Stock and sales quantities are numeric(12,3).
  • Refunding part of a weighed item (0.5 kg of 1.25 kg) is allowed.

Variants

  • A product is the idea (“Cotton T-shirt”). A variant is the sellable thing (“Red / M”).
  • Price, barcode, SKU and stock are stored on the variant.
  • Every product has at least one variant. A simple product has a single “Default” variant, so checkout code is the same for everything.
  • Sales and stock always refer to the variant.

Key flows

Checkout (the most important flow)

Build the cart in the browser

Scan, search or tap items. Enter weight for loose items. Nothing is saved yet.

Send items and payment to the server

Only variant IDs, quantities, discounts requested and payments. Plus a unique request key.

Server starts a database transaction

Everything below happens inside it. Any failure cancels the whole thing.

Lock and check stock

Lock the variant rows (lockForUpdate). If any quantity is more than available stock, stop with a clear message. This is the “blocked at zero” rule.

Work out the totals

Look up current prices, compute line totals, tax, discount, cash rounding. Check the payments cover the total.

Write the records

Create the sale, sale items (with snapshots), payments, stock movements (negative), update cached stock, assign the next receipt number.

Commit and show the receipt

Return the receipt page, open a fresh empty cart. If the same request key arrives again, return the same receipt instead of making a second sale.

Void

Manager only, and only within a configurable window (for example same day). The sale is marked voided, stock is put back through new void movements, and the user and reason are recorded. The original rows stay in place.

Refund

Creates a refund with refund_items (partial amounts and partial weights allowed). Returns stock through new movements, records who and why, and records the money paid out in the shift.

Hardware and running it for real

Barcode scanner

Most USB scanners act like a keyboard that types the number and presses Enter. They work in a browser with no extra software. We only need an always-focused search box.

Receipts

Start with a print-friendly receipt page (browser print dialog, CSS sized for 80 mm paper). Direct thermal-printer commands (ESC/POS) can come later if needed.

Weighing scale

Version 1: the cashier types the weight. Later: read a connected scale or scale-printed barcodes.

Cash drawer and card terminal

Out of scope for now. Terminals usually involve fees, which conflicts with the zero-budget rule, so cards are recorded manually with a reference number.

Running it in a real shop (later)

  • Easiest and free: install Laravel and PostgreSQL on one PC in the shop. Other devices on the same network open the app in a browser. No internet needed.
  • Free hosting tiers exist but are limited and change often. Check current limits before relying on one, and do not put real customer data on a service you have not reviewed.
  • Backups are mandatory before real use: a scheduled pg_dump to another drive (and ideally a USB copy kept off-site), plus a tested restore.
  • Use a UPS on the shop PC because of power cuts.

Roadmap

Phases are in order but not dated. Add dates once you know how many hours per week each of you can give. Sizes are rough guesses only. Tick boxes are saved in your browser.

Phase 0: Foundation Small

Goal: both of us can run the same empty app locally and ship a change through a pull request.

Done when: a fresh clone runs on both machines, CI is green, and each of us has had one pull request reviewed and merged.

Phase 1: MVP, sell something Large

Goal: a cashier can log in, sell products (including weighed ones) for cash, and stock goes down.

Done when: we can run a pretend shop day with seeded products: sell items and weights, print receipts, see correct stock afterwards, and the automated tests prove the totals.

Phase 2: Run a shop day Large

Goal: the things a manager needs to trust the system at closing time.

Done when: a manager can open a shift, let a cashier trade, handle a refund, close the shift and see a report whose numbers match the cash in the drawer.

Phase 3: Real-world polish Medium to large

Goal: comfortable and safe enough for an actual small shop.

Done when: a real shop can use it for a full week without us touching the code, and a backup has been restored successfully at least once.

Phase 4: Later and ideas Not committed

Revisit once Phase 3 is done.

  • Loyalty points and promotions
  • Multiple branches
  • Direct thermal printer support (ESC/POS) and cash drawer kick
  • Scale integration and scale-printed barcodes
  • Tablet and touch-friendly layout
  • Offline-capable mode
  • Remote access or hosting, only if a free and trustworthy option exists
  • Online payment integrations (usually have fees)
  • Expiry dates and batch tracking (useful for food and medicine)

Team workflow

Branches

  • main is protected. Changes arrive only by pull request.
  • develop is the working branch.
  • Make a branch from develop: feature/checkout-screen, fix/rounding, docs/roadmap.
  • Open a pull request into develop.

Pull requests

  • Small: one feature or fix per pull request.
  • The other person reviews before merging.
  • Tests and Pint must pass (CI checks this).
  • Describe what changed and how to try it.

Definition of done (for any task)

  • It works when you run it locally from a fresh migration and seed.
  • Tests exist for the money, stock and permission parts, and they pass.
  • No secrets, .env or personal data in the diff.
  • These docs are updated if the design changed.

Glossary

POS
Point of Sale. The software at the till where a customer pays.
SKU
Stock Keeping Unit. A shop's own code for an item or variant.
Barcode
The number on the product that a scanner reads. Can be printed by the maker or by the shop.
Variant
One specific sellable version of a product (for example Red, size M).
Unit
How an item is measured and priced: piece, kilogram, litre, dozen.
Paisa
One hundredth of a rupee. We store money as whole paisa.
Ledger
A list where you only add entries, never edit old ones. Used for stock movements.
Void
Cancel a whole sale because it was a mistake.
Refund
Give money back for goods returned. Can be partial.
Shift
One cashier's session at the till, with opening and closing cash counts.
Tender
The money a customer hands over. Change is tender minus the amount due.
Tax-inclusive
The shelf price already contains the tax.
Tax-exclusive
Tax is added on top of the shelf price at checkout.
GST
General Sales Tax (federal sales tax on goods in Pakistan).
FBR
Federal Board of Revenue, Pakistan's federal tax authority.
Udhaar
Selling on credit, with the customer paying later.
Migration
A file that creates or changes database tables. Shared through Git so both machines match.
Seeder
A script that fills the database with demo data.
Transaction
A group of database writes that either all succeed or all fail.
Idempotency key
A unique ID sent with a request so a repeat does the work only once.
Eloquent
Laravel's way of working with database tables as PHP objects.
Inertia
A bridge that lets Laravel serve Vue or React pages without building a separate API.
Policy
A Laravel class that decides who is allowed to do a given action.
CI
Continuous Integration. Automatic checks that run on every pull request.
Pull request (PR)
A proposal to merge your branch into another, reviewed before it lands.
Milestone
A GitHub grouping of issues, used here for roadmap phases.
ESC/POS
The command language many thermal receipt printers understand.
UPS
Uninterruptible power supply: a battery that keeps the PC running through a power cut.
