1. Summary

A point of sale and stock system that several shops can use (multi-tenant), where each shop can later have several branches. We build the backend first. The first release serves one shop with one branch: register products and vendors, receive stock with a GRN, sell for cash, close a shift and see sales reports.

First release (MVP)

  • Shops, branches, users and roles
  • Categories, products, variants, pack sizes, barcodes
  • Vendors and GRN
  • Stock per branch with a stock ledger and manual adjustment
  • Shifts, cash in and cash out
  • Cash sales with line discounts, receipt numbers
  • Sales reports by day, week and month

Next release

  • Void and refund
  • Vendor return
  • Customer credit (udhaar)
  • Order-level discount
  • Other payment methods (card, bank transfer, wallet) and split payment
  • Owner portal

Later

  • Audit log screen and audit reports
  • Low-stock reminders
  • Urdu and Roman Urdu screens and receipts
  • Tax and FBR

The first release still records who did each sensitive action and when. Only the audit screens wait.

4. Architecture

Multi-tenancy and branches (Proposed design)

  • One application and one database. Every business table has tenant_id.
  • Stock, shifts, sales and GRNs also have branch_id.
  • A global query scope limits every query to the current tenant.
  • Unique rules are per tenant, for example product code and barcode.
  • Automated tests prove that one shop cannot read or change another shop's data.

Backend first

Business rules live in Action classes (for example PostGoodsReceipt, CompleteSale, CloseShift). Each phase is verified with feature tests against PostgreSQL and with seeded demo data. The React screens call the same Actions through thin controllers.

Owner portal

The portal records which shops use the product and what each one owes us. We enter payments by hand. It takes no online payment. It uses its own user table and login, separate from shop users. Locally it runs on a separate host name such as owner.localhost.

Languages

All screen and receipt text goes through Laravel translation files from the first day (en, ur-Latn, ur). Products can store an Urdu name. Urdu also needs a right-to-left layout.

Cost risk

Development stays free and local. A multi-shop product with its own domain needs hosting and a domain before real clients can use it, and both usually cost money. This conflicts with the zero-budget rule and needs a decision before the first real client.

6. Catalog

  • Category: registered once, then selected on products. Sub-categories are optional.
  • Product: a code that is unique within the shop (generated automatically, can be changed), name, optional Urdu name, category.
  • Variant: the thing that is stocked. It has a base unit (piece, kg, litre). A simple product has one "Default" variant.
  • Pack size: a way of selling a variant, with its own price and barcode.

Stock is always counted in the base unit.

Sell asBase quantityPrice (example)
1 pencil1Rs 15
Half pack12Rs 170
Box24Rs 320

Selling one box from 100 pencils leaves 76 pencils.

The base unit sits on the variant, so a 1 L bottle and a 5 L tin of oil are two variants counted in pieces. Loose oil is a variant counted in litres.

7. Purchasing

Vendors

Code, name, phone, address.

Goods Received Note (GRN)

  1. Select the vendor and branch.
  2. Enter the vendor invoice number and date.
  3. Add lines: product, pack size, quantity, cost.
  4. Save as a draft and check it.
  5. Post it.

Posting happens in one transaction: GRN number assigned, stock increased, stock ledger rows written, average cost updated. A posted GRN is never edited or deleted.

Weighted average cost

QuantityCost per piece
Existing stock100Rs 10.00
New GRN48Rs 12.00
Result148Rs 10.65

Average and unit costs are stored with four decimals. If existing stock is zero, the average becomes the new GRN cost.

8. Selling

Shifts

A cashier opens a shift with a cash count. Only one open shift per cashier. Every sale belongs to a shift. Cash taken out of or put into the drawer is recorded with a reason.

Expected cash = opening cash + cash sales + cash in − cash out. The next release adds cash refunds and udhaar collections.

Checkout

  1. The client sends pack IDs, quantities, discounts, payment and a unique request key.
  2. The server finds the prices and calculates all totals itself.
  3. In one transaction it locks the branch stock rows (always in the same order), checks stock, writes the sale, items, payment and stock ledger rows, and assigns the receipt number.
  4. A repeat of the same request key returns the first receipt.

Lines for the same variant in different pack sizes are added together before the stock check.

Sales reports

By day, week and month: number of sales, total sales, discounts, per cashier and per branch, and gross profit for managers and owners. Dates follow the shop's time zone (Proposed: Asia/Karachi, week starts Monday).

9. Customer credit (udhaar), next release

  • A customer has a credit limit.
  • A ledger holds debit entries (credit sale) and credit entries (payment received).
  • Ledger entries are added, never edited. The balance is calculated from them.
  • A sale on credit above the limit needs a manager.

11. Core rules

  1. Money is stored as whole paisa. Unit costs use four decimals. No floating-point numbers.
  2. Quantities use exact decimals.
  3. Posted sales and GRNs are never edited or deleted.
  4. Sale lines keep copies of name, pack size, price and cost.
  5. Every stock change has a stock ledger row, in the same transaction.
  6. A sale or GRN is saved completely or not at all.
  7. Receipt and GRN numbers come from a locked counter row per branch, so there are no gaps.
  8. A request key stops double submission.
  9. The server never trusts totals from the client.
  10. Sensitive actions record who and when.
  11. No secrets or real shop data in Git. The repository is public.

13. Differences from the current documents

  • Multi-tenancy and branches replace the single-store design.
  • Inertia + React replaces the Vue proposal.
  • Backend is built before the screens.
  • Pack sizes are added. Price and barcode move from the variant to the pack size. Base unit moves from the product to the variant. The variant sku is replaced by the product code.
  • Vendors and GRN move into the first release.
  • Shifts move into the first release, because every sale belongs to a shift.
  • Tax moves out of the first release.
  • _cents column names become _paisa.
  • stock_qty moves to a per-branch stock table.
  • Added: cash movements, cost snapshot on sale lines, gap-free number counters, shop time zone.
  • Refunds, when built, record the shift and payment method, and cannot exceed the quantity sold.
  • Order discounts, when built, are split across lines in proportion, with the remainder on the last line.
  • Use the current Laravel starter kit instead of Breeze, and turn off public registration.
  • Suggested: protect develop as well as main.

14. Open questions

  1. The first release has no void and no vendor return. A wrong sale or GRN can only be handled with a stock adjustment, which does not correct sales totals or average cost. Is that acceptable, or do these two move into the first release?
  2. Is udhaar fine in the next release, or is it needed in the first one?
  3. Do we buy from vendors on credit? If yes, we also need a vendor ledger.
  4. Do any products need expiry dates or batches?
  5. Does the owner portal only record what shops owe us, with no online payment?
  6. Do we create new shops ourselves, or can a shop register itself?
  7. What kind of shop is the first target?
